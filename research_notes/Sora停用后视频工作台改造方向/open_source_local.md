## Q1. Which open-weight video models are state of the art as of Oct 2026, under what licenses, with what capabilities and quality?

### Takeaway
As of October 2026 the strongest open-weight video models are **MiniMax H3** (Aug 2026, 33B), **Lightricks LTX-2.5** (Aug 2026, 22B, native audio) and **Wan 2.2** (Jul 2025, Apache-2.0). H3 ranks about #4 overall on Artificial Analysis's T2V v2.0 board, but its license excludes the US, EU, UK and South Korea. LTX-2.5 scores about 190 Elo lower and is free only for companies under US$10M ARR. Wan 2.2 is the only top-tier model under a fully permissive license, but it is now 14 months old. Alibaba stopped releasing open weights for its flagship models after Wan 2.2: Wan 2.5, 2.6, 2.7 and 3.0 are all API-only.

**MiniMax H3 (Hailuo 3.0): the new open-weights leader (Aug 2026)**
- H3 was announced July 31, 2026 (WAIC 2026). The official MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-H3 Hugging Face repo is dated Aug 3, 2026 and uses the "MiniMax H3 Community License Agreement". — [HF blog explainer](https://huggingface.co/blog/ResterChed/minimax-h3-hailuo-3-0); [MiniMax official blog](https://www.minimax.io/blog/minimax-h3); [aiweekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/minimax-posts-h3-a-33b-omni-modal-video-model-to-hugging-face)
- License terms, all from secondary write-ups: deployment in the **US, EU, UK and South Korea is excluded**; organisations above about $20M annual revenue need authorisation; training other models on H3 outputs is prohibited. One write-up argues that using outputs inside the excluded territories is also prohibited. — [explainx](https://explainx.ai/blog/minimax-h3-open-video-model-hailuo-july-2026); [atoms.dev](https://atoms.dev/blog/minimax-h3-open-weights-multimodal-video-model); [superpowerdaily](https://superpowerdaily.com/posts/minimax-h3-opens-its-video-weights-but-not-where-many-developers-work)
- Only H3-Base is downloadable. H3-Context-IR and H3-Regenerate-2K stay closed, and higher-resolution output is tied to the hosted API (conflicting naming: ComfyUI guides describe "FL2VA" and "Ref2VA" variants). — [atoms.dev](https://atoms.dev/blog/minimax-h3-open-weights-multimodal-video-model); [comfyui-wiki news](https://comfyui-wiki.com/en/news/2026-08-03-minimax-h3-open-weights-comfyui)
- Specs: about 33.1B dense single-stream "Omni Transformer" with a Qwen3-VL-32B text encoder. Clips are up to 15 s at 2K (1440-px short edge) or a 768p tier, fixed 24 fps, with native stereo audio. Modes are T2V, I2V, first+last frame, and omni reference (up to 9 images, 3 video clips and 3 audio clips), plus editing and multi-shot. Prompts can run to 7,000 characters. — [Morphic spec sheet](https://morphic.com/resources/models/minimax-h3); [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/08/01/minimax-releases-minimax-h3-an-omni-modal-video-model-that-generates-15-second-2k-clips-with-native-stereo-audio/); [RunComfy](https://www.runcomfy.com/models/minimax/minimax-h3); [fal](https://fal.ai/minimax-h3)
- H3 is also sold as a hosted API through fal, RunComfy and others. — [fal](https://fal.ai/minimax-h3)

- LTX-2.5 was released Aug 11, 2026: 22B parameters, open weights on a gated Hugging Face repo, day-0 ComfyUI support, and a hosted API. — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/ltx-2-5-can-generate-a-10-second-ai-video-from-an-image-in-just-6-8-seconds-on-nvidia-superchips-and-its-open-weights); [ComfyUI blog](https://blog.comfy.org/p/ltx-25-day-0-support-in-comfyui); [LTX model page](https://ltx.io/model/ltx-2-5)

Audio is generated in the same pass at 48 kHz, and multi-shot is native. Through the API, Fast runs 2–20 s from 720p to 4K at 24/25/48/50 fps (over 10 s only at 720p/1080p, 24/25 fps). Pro runs 2–10 s; sources conflict on whether Pro reaches 4K. LoRA and full fine-tuning are supported. — [LTX docs](https://docs.ltx.io/models/ltx-2-5); [fal LTX-2.5](https://fal.ai/ltx-2.5); [OrcaRouter](https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/ltx-2-5-explained)

### Inferences
- For a globally distributed MIT tool, the "safe default" backends are **Wan 2.2 (Apache-2.0)** and **LTX-2.5 (free under $10M ARR, AI-output labelling required)**. H3 and HunyuanVideo should be opt-in, with a visible license notice, because their territory exclusions (US/EU/UK/KR for H3; EU/UK/KR for Hunyuan) cover large parts of the likely user base. For a maintainer and users located in mainland China, H3 is likely usable under $20M revenue, but a lawyer should read the text.
- The open-vs-closed gap has narrowed at the very top (H3 is #4 overall), but the best permissively licensed model (Wan 2.2) is a mid-2025 model. Users moving from Sora 2 Pro to a permissive local model should expect a visible quality drop.
- The app's "first-frame reference image" feature maps cleanly to I2V in Wan 2.2 (I2V-A14B / TI2V-5B), LTX-2.5, H3 and HunyuanVideo 1.5. LTX-2.5 and H3 also offer first+last frame. Native audio (which Sora 2 had) is available only in LTX-2.x, H3 and SkyReels V3 A2V, not in Wan 2.2.
- Alibaba's pattern (open 2.1/2.2, then closed 2.5–3.0 while still open-sourcing side models) suggests the "no vendor shutdown risk" argument holds for **already-downloaded weights**, not for future upgrades.

### Gaps
- No LICENSE text was read directly (Hugging Face was blocked). The H3 revenue threshold and territory list, the LTX-2.5 terms, and SkyReels V3's license all need direct verification.
- I found no VBench scores for the 2026 models (H3, LTX-2.5). VBench comparisons in the literature are mostly 2025 or earlier.
- I could not confirm whether anything newer than LTX-2.5 and H3 (for example a MAGI-2 full release or a HunyuanVideo 2.x open release) shipped in September or early October 2026. One search for "open source video model September 2026" found nothing.
- The AA snapshot date for the v2.0 numbers is unknown. Check the live board.
- The exact parameter counts for Step-Video-T2V and Open-Sora 2.0 were not re-verified. They are not decision-relevant.

## Q2. Hardware requirements and speed (NVIDIA consumer GPUs and Apple Silicon)

### Takeaway
On NVIDIA hardware, a 5 s 720p clip is practical. Wan 2.2 14B with a distilled 4–8-step LoRA takes roughly **1–5 min on an RTX 4090 and about 1.5–2 min on a 5090**; at default 50 steps it takes about 9+ min. LTX-2.x is several times faster than Wan. H3 needs a 32 GB card and takes roughly 2.5–9 min per clip on a 5090. **On a Mac, only LTX-2.x (via MLX ports) is borderline practical**, at about 1–4 min per short clip on a 64–128 GB M4 Max. Wan 14B and HunyuanVideo on Apple Silicon take tens of minutes to more than an hour per clip, and FP8 checkpoints do not run on Metal.

### Cited Findings

**Wan 2.2 on NVIDIA (speed numbers vary widely; conditions noted)**
- Wan 2.2 14B, 720p, 5 s: about **2 min 40 s on an RTX 4090 and about 1 min 45 s on an RTX 5090**, about 15 GB VRAM. Step count and LoRA use are not stated. — [bestgpuforai](https://bestgpuforai.com/articles/rtx-5090-vs-4090-for-video-gen/) (unverified conditions)
- Another guide gives 3–5 min for 720p 5 s Wan 2.2 on a 4090. — [localaimaster Wan guide](https://localaimaster.com/blog/wan-video-generation-guide)
- At default settings, the A14B models take about **9 min** for a 5 s 720p clip. — [MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/what-is-wan-2-2-video-open-source)
- RTX 5090, Wan 2.2 14B at 832×480, 81 frames, 6 steps: **125 s** (warm runs). The same benchmark found LTX-2.3 22B about **5.7× faster** than Wan 2.2 for I2V. — [Zenn benchmark](https://zenn.dev/toki_mwc/articles/ltx23-vs-wan22-i2v-benchmark-rtx5090?locale=en)
- With FusionX plus LightX2V LoRAs (4 steps per expert, 8 total), one user reports about 170–200 s per render (GPU and resolution not stated). The LightX2V distilled models advertise 4-step inference and about 2× speed-up over ComfyUI. — [HF forum thread](https://discuss.huggingface.co/t/nvidia-geforce-rtx-5060-ti-and-wan2-2-model/170645); [lightx2v/Wan2.2-Distill-Models](https://huggingface.co/lightx2v/Wan2.2-Distill-Models)
- Wan 2.2 5B on a 4090 at 50 steps: 9.92 s/step, 8 min 13 s total. — [computingforgeeks](https://computingforgeeks.com/run-wan-video-generation-locally/) (attribution approximate)
- Chinese community tests (CSDN): Wan 2.2 14B 5 s on a 4090 in about 59 s versus 142 s on a 3090 (config unspecified). TI2V-5B 720p 5 s on a 4090 took 540 s at 22.8 GB peak. One promotional piece claims 30 s. — [CSDN 3090 vs 4090](https://adg.csdn.net/695259e55b9f5f31781ba89e.html); [CSDN 4090 vs A10](https://blog.csdn.net/weixin_35826166/article/details/157639636) (conflicting; configs differ)
- VRAM: the official Wan 2.2 14B command assumes at least 80 GB VRAM. Community FP8 and GGUF builds plus offloading bring 14B down to 24 GB and lower; 5B fits in 24 GB. — [Wan2.2 GitHub](https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.2); [arulps/wan-video-serverless](https://github.com/arulps/wan-video-serverless); [willitrunai VRAM guide](https://willitrunai.com/blog/wan-2-2-vram-requirements); [wan27.org GGUF guide](https://wan27.org/blog/wan-2-2-gguf-guide)

**LTX-2.5 / 2.3 VRAM**
- Lightricks states a 16 GB floor. LTX's own guide recommends 32 GB with FP8 (5090-class). Hokai says full precision needs 32 GB or more, FP8 cuts that by about 40%, and community GGUF builds target 12–16 GB. RunPod says comfortable production use starts around 48 GB, even with the distilled checkpoint. — [LTX model page](https://ltx.io/model/ltx-2-5); [LTX open-source guide](https://ltx.io/blog/open-source-video-generation-models-guide); [Hokai](https://hokai.io/hub/models/ltx-2.5); [RunPod LTX-2.5 blog](https://www.runpod.io/blog/ltx-2-5-the-open-weights-world-model-built-for-speed-and-how-to-run-it-on-runpod) (conflicting)

**MiniMax H3 VRAM and speed**
- ComfyUI's day-0 support prunes about 40% of the parameters (modulation weights) into a lookup table and uses int8 quantization, cutting the footprint from **123.6 GB to 42.5 GB**. — relayed by [kingy.ai ComfyUI guide](https://kingy.ai/ai/ai-guides/minimax-h3-comfyui-local-guide/) and [localaimaster H3 setup](https://localaimaster.com/blog/minimax-h3-local-setup-guide) (secondary)
- On an RTX 5090 (32 GB), 36 runs peaked at about 31.8 GB. A pruned NVFP4 build peaked at 26.9 GiB. Raw weights on a 4090 peaked around 46 GB for long clips (i.e., they need offload). One guide treats 12 GB as a heavy-offload floor (32 GB system RAM) and 24 GB as where the pruned int8 build fits. — [wan2-7.io H3 requirements](https://wan2-7.io/blog/minimax-h3-local-requirements/); [ai-muninn NVFP4](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/minimax-h3-nvfp4-rtx5090); [instasd VRAM guide](https://www.instasd.com/post/comfyui-vram-offloading-guide)

**HunyuanVideo 1.5 VRAM**
- About 14 GB VRAM with offloading. Estimates are about 4.8 GB at 4-bit, 9.6 GB at 8-bit and 19.2 GB at 16-bit. Wan2GP claims 6 GB, and community GGUF builds run on 8 GB. — [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/tencent/HunyuanVideo-1.5); [immers.cloud](https://en.immers.cloud/ai/tencent/HunyuanVideo-1.5) **[>3 mo]**

- HunyuanVideo in ComfyUI on an M3 Max 128 GB: about 155 s/it, about 100 GB of RAM, about **60 min for 73 frames at 848×480**. — [HF discussion](https://huggingface.co/tencent/HunyuanVideo/discussions/13) **[>3 mo]**

- **Draw Things** added Wan 2.2 on Aug 4, 2025. The 14B model uses about 20 GB RAM for 1024×576 at 81 frames (SVDQuant). A 2026 guide claims Draw Things is 20–40% faster than ComfyUI on Mac (Metal FlashAttention v2; no benchmark shown) and recommends 24 GB or more for video. — [Draw Things wiki: Wan 2.2](https://wiki.drawthings.ai/wiki/Wan_2.2); [heyuan110 guide](https://www.heyuan110.com/posts/ai/2026-02-15-draw-things-ultimate-guide/) (speed claim unverified)
- **LTX-2.x via MLX (community ports, not Lightricks)**: janishar/ltx-2-mlx did a 2 s 704×448 clip in about **40 s** (20.9 GiB peak) and a 5 s 1280×704 clip in about **207 s**, though that run hit swap at 75.9 GiB. - A native macOS app (community) claims to run LTX-2, 2.3, 2.5 and MiniMax H3 on Apple Silicon. — [james-see/ltx-video-mac](https://github.com/james-see/ltx-video-mac) (not tested)
- **LTX Desktop on Mac** (sources conflict): the GitHub README says local generation runs on MPS for Apple Silicon Macs with at least 15 GB free RAM, otherwise API-only. The help centre (about 3 months old) says local GPU inference is not supported on Mac and an API key is required. The product page says 48 GB or more for full local generation, and 32 GB is limited to 540p/5 s. — [LTX-Desktop GitHub](https://github.com/Lightricks/LTX-Desktop); [LTX help: system requirements](https://help.ltx.io/hc/en-us/articles/37089000020242-LTX-Desktop-system-requirements); [ltx.io/ltx-desktop](https://ltx.io/ltx-desktop) (conflicting)
- One guide says Macs under 64 GB unified memory are "not recommended" for LTX-2.3. Its 4–6 min estimate for a 6 s 4K clip on an M4 Max 128 GB is unverified. — [halmob](https://halmob.com/blog/ltx-23-mac-native-ai-video-generation-guide) (unverified)

### Inferences
- **The practical tier for a hobbyist is an RTX 4090 (24 GB) or 5090 (32 GB)**. 24 GB covers Wan 2.2 (FP8/GGUF), HunyuanVideo 1.5 and LTX-2.x quantised. 32 GB is needed to run H3 without heavy offload. A 3090 works but takes about 2–2.5× longer than a 4090 (single CSDN data point).
- With distilled LoRAs on a 4090, about 2–3 min per 5 s 720p Wan clip gives roughly 20–30 clips per hour per GPU. A 50,000-prompt batch (the old Batch API ceiling) would take on the order of **1,700–2,500 GPU-hours (about 70–100 days on one GPU)**. Bulk mode only makes sense with multi-GPU or cloud fan-out. (Arithmetic from the cited per-clip times.)
- **The Mac bundle should not promise on-device generation for Wan or Hunyuan.** At best, offer an "experimental Mac local" path for LTX-2.x via MLX (or Draw Things) on Macs with 64 GB or more. Default the Mac build to driving a remote NVIDIA box (LAN PC or cloud pod).
- Speed numbers in this space are not comparable across sources. A redesigned app should time each job itself and show measured throughput.

### Gaps
- I found no measured M4 Max or M3 Ultra timing for Wan 2.2 (5B or 14B), and no Draw Things video benchmark with numbers.
- I found no clean, like-for-like 4090 versus 5090 versus 3090 benchmark at 720p, 81 frames, 4-step LightX2V with identical settings. The wan27.org GGUF guide claims one, but the numbers were not retrievable.
- I found no reliable consumer-GPU timing for LTX-2.5 (only the GB200 figure and LTX-2.3 relative speed).
- RTX 3090 data for H3 and LTX-2.5 was not found.

## Q3. Local inference tooling: how a third-party frontend (like Sora2App) can submit and collect jobs

### Takeaway
There are two realistic integration targets. (1) **ComfyUI's native HTTP+WebSocket API** (`POST /prompt`, then `/ws` or `GET /history/{id}`, then `GET /view`) with API-format workflow JSON templates. It has the broadest model coverage, including day-0 H3 and LTX-2.5, and runs on Mac via MPS, but has no auth and no built-in text batching. (2) **OpenAI-compatible `/v1/videos` servers (SGLang Diffusion, vLLM-Omni)**. These implement a subset of the very OpenAI Videos API Sora2App already speaks, so the existing create, poll and download code could be reused almost unchanged, but they are Linux/NVIDIA-centric. Wan2GP offers a documented Python and headless queue API, but no HTTP API was found. Draw Things has an HTTP server, but video over HTTP is unconfirmed.

### Cited Findings

**ComfyUI native API**
- `POST /prompt` validates a workflow, adds it to the execution queue, and returns `prompt_id` and queue position (or `error`/`node_errors`). Execution is asynchronous: poll `/history/{prompt_id}` or subscribe to the WebSocket. — [ComfyUI docs: server routes](https://docs.comfy.org/development/comfyui-server/comms_routes); [Runflow guide](https://www.runflow.io/blog/comfyui-api-developer-guide)
- `/ws` streams execution status, per-node progress and previews. A `client_id` correlates events to submissions. The official example treats "executing with node=null for our prompt_id" as completion, then reads `/history`. `GET /view?filename&subfolder&type` returns output bytes. `/object_info` lists all nodes (including custom ones). Interrupt and queue-management routes exist. — [ComfyUI docs](https://docs.comfy.org/development/comfyui-server/comms_routes); [official websockets_api_example.py](https://github.com/Comfy-Org/ComfyUI/blob/master/script_examples/websockets_api_example.py)
- The **API workflow format** differs from the UI save file. It is a map of node IDs to `{class_type, inputs}`, where inputs are constants or links `[node_id, output_index]`, with no layout data. Export it via File → Export Workflow (API); older builds used a Dev-mode "Save (API Format)" button. — [ComfyUI docs: workflow API format](https://docs.comfy.org/development/api-development/workflow-api-format); [DeepWiki](https://deepwiki.com/Comfy-Org/ComfyUI/7.3-workflow-json-format)
- **No authentication by default.** Every endpoint, including uploads and VRAM freeing, is exposed on whatever interface it binds to. — [Runflow endpoints reference](https://www.runflow.io/blog/comfyui-api-endpoints)
- Ports: the portable/manual build defaults to **8188**; a community wiki says ComfyUI Desktop uses **8000**. A Desktop feature request says Desktop does not expose a user-configurable listening port. — [comfyui-wiki server config](https://comfyui-wiki.com/en/interface/settings/server-config); [Comfy-Org/desktop issue #1734](https://github.com/Comfy-Org/desktop/issues/1734) (conflicting)
- Comfy's hosted cloud uses `/api/prompt` with the same API-format workflow, plus an API key in `extra_data` for partner nodes. — [Comfy Cloud API reference](https://docs.comfy.org/development/cloud/api-reference)
- Stock ComfyUI lacks native text-batch input. Batching is done by custom nodes or client-side loops: ComfyUI-Simple-Prompt-Batcher (one prompt per line, one queue submission, models stay loaded), KikoTools "Batch Prompts" (file split by `---`, index auto-increments), and **comfy-batcher**, a CLI that reads a text file of prompts and queues them via the API using a `.map` file of node fields. — [ComfyUI issue #1561](https://github.com/comfyanonymous/ComfyUI/issues/1561); [Simple-Prompt-Batcher](https://github.com/ai-joe-git/ComfyUI-Simple-Prompt-Batcher); [comfy.org batch workflow](https://comfy.org/workflows/utility_text_lists_select_prompt-2da831d21d09/); [comfy-batcher](https://github.com/rbbrdckybk/comfy-batcher)
- Comfy publishes a beta TypeScript SDK (`npm i @comfyorg/sdk`). — [comfy.org workflow page](https://comfy.org/workflows/utility_text_lists_select_prompt-2da831d21d09/)
- **SaladTechnologies/comfyui-api**: a stateless REST wrapper that launches ComfyUI as a child process. It listens on port 3000 (`PORT` env), serves Swagger at `/docs`, returns base64 outputs inline or delivers them asynchronously via webhook (Runflow mentions `/webhook_v2` and S3 upload; unverified), and is built to scale horizontally. — [comfyui-api GitHub](https://github.com/SaladTechnologies/comfyui-api); [Salad docs](https://docs.salad.com/container-engine/how-to-guides/ai-machine-learning/deploy-stable-diffusion-comfy)
- Open WebUI documents a ComfyUI integration, an example of a third-party frontend driving ComfyUI with exported workflow JSON. — [Open WebUI docs](https://docs.openwebui.com/features/chat-conversations/image-generation-and-editing/comfyui/)
- ComfyUI had **day-0 support for LTX-2.5** (launch partnership) and **MiniMax H3** (pruned int8 build). — [ComfyUI blog LTX-2.5](https://blog.comfy.org/p/ltx-25-day-0-support-in-comfyui); [comfyui-wiki H3](https://comfyui-wiki.com/en/news/2026-08-03-minimax-h3-open-weights-comfyui)

**OpenAI-compatible local video servers (closest to the old Sora code path)**
- **vLLM-Omni** exposes async video jobs at `/v1/videos` (POST to create, GET by id for status) and a synchronous `/v1/videos/sync` that returns raw MP4 bytes. Each server hosts one model (`vllm serve <model> --omni`). The quick start serves Wan2.2 T2V on port 8091. — [vLLM-Omni Videos API docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/projects/vllm-omni/en/latest/serving/videos_api/)
- NVIDIA Dynamo wraps vLLM-Omni. Its tested models include Wan2.1 T2V 1.3B, Wan2.2 T2V A14B, TI2V 5B and I2V A14B. — [NVIDIA Dynamo vLLM-Omni](https://docs.nvidia.com/dynamo/v1.2.1/backends/v-llm/v-llm-omni)
- vLLM publishes serve recipes for MiniMax-H3, including RTX 4090, RTX 5090, DGX Spark and others. — [vLLM recipes: MiniMax-H3](https://recipes.vllm.ai/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-H3)
- **SGLang Diffusion** implements "a subset of the OpenAI Videos API under `/v1/videos`". The **official OpenAI Python client** pointed at `http://localhost:30010/v1` can call `videos.create`, poll, and download the MP4. I2V/TI2V (Wan2.1 I2V, LTX-2.3 two-stage) takes an image via multipart upload or a URL. `--served-model-name` gives a stable alias. — [SGLang OpenAI API docs](https://docs.sglang.io/docs/sglang-diffusion/api/openai_api); [LMSYS launch blog](https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2025-11-07-sglang-diffusion/)
- SGLang Diffusion lists support for Wan, Hunyuan, Qwen-Image and FLUX, and for NVIDIA, AMD, Intel XPU, Ascend, Apple Silicon and Moore Threads hardware (Apple Silicon video performance not verified). It has a MiniMax-H3 cookbook entry. — [SGLang Diffusion docs](https://docs.sglang.io/docs/sglang-diffusion); [SGLang H3 cookbook](https://docs.sglang.io/cookbook/diffusion/MiniMax/MiniMax-H3)

**Wan2GP (WanGP, "for the GPU poor")**
- Supports MiniMax H3, LTX-2.3/2.5, Wan 2.1/2.2, Hunyuan Video, LTX Video and Flux. It targets low VRAM (for example, HunyuanVideo 1.5 claimed on 6 GB). — [Wan2GP GitHub](https://github.com/deepbeepmeep/Wan2GP); [HF HunyuanVideo-1.5 card](https://huggingface.co/tencent/HunyuanVideo-1.5)
- Batch support: build a queue in the web UI, "Save Queue", then process it headless from the CLI (exact flag conflicts across guides). A Python API offers `submit_task(settings)` and `submit_manifest(list)`, an MCP v2 interface, per-task statuses (queued/running/cancelling), cancel semantics, and a lock so only one generation runs on the GPU at a time. — [Wan2GP docs/API.md](https://github.com/deepbeepmeep/Wan2GP/blob/main/docs/API.md); [DeepWiki task queue](https://deepwiki.com/deepbeepmeep/Wan2GP/3.2-task-queue-system)

**SwarmUI, Draw Things, LTX Desktop, diffusers**
- **SwarmUI** uses ComfyUI as its backend engine and supports Wan, LTX-2, MiniMax H3 and others. Its maintainer describes I2V as a "trick" layered on the image workflow. No documented batch video API was found in this session. — [SwarmUI video model support](https://github.com/mcmonkeyprojects/SwarmUI/blob/master/docs/Video%20Model%20Support.md); [SwarmUI discussion #716](https://github.com/mcmonkeyprojects/SwarmUI/discussions/716)
- **Draw Things** (Mac/iOS): the HTTP API server is off by default; default port 7860, A1111-style `/sdapi/v1/...` paths, with gRPC also available. The glossary lists text-to-video, but **no HTTP route for video jobs was confirmed**. Batch work goes through its JavaScript scripting API. Draw Things has also announced "MediaGenerationKit" for app integration (details not retrieved). — [Draw Things glossary](https://wiki.drawthings.ai/wiki/Glossary); [heyuan110 API guide](https://www.heyuan110.com/posts/ai/2026-02-16-claude-code-draw-things-workflow/); [Scripting Basics](https://wiki.drawthings.ai/wiki/Scripting_Basics); [MediaGenerationKit](https://releases.drawthings.ai/p/introducing-mediagenerationkit-hybrid)
- **LTX Desktop**: an open-source (Apache) desktop app for LTX models. Its local Mac generation status conflicts across sources (see Q2). — [LTX-Desktop GitHub](https://github.com/Lightricks/LTX-Desktop)
- **diffusers** has pipelines for HunyuanVideo and FramePack, among others (Python library, not a server). — [diffusers HunyuanVideo](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/en/api/pipelines/hunyuan_video); [diffusers FramePack](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/main/api/pipelines/framepack)

### Inferences
- **Recommended architecture for the redesign: a pluggable "backend adapter" layer.**
  1. **Adapter A, "OpenAI-compatible Videos" (lowest effort):** keep the existing Sora request lifecycle (create, poll status, download content) and make the base URL and model name configurable. Point it at SGLang Diffusion or vLLM-Omni on a Linux/NVIDIA box (local, LAN or cloud). This preserves most of the current zero-dependency Node code, and it also future-proofs against any other vendor implementing `/v1/videos`.
  2. **Adapter B, ComfyUI (widest model coverage, works with Desktop and Mac):** ship a small set of API-format workflow templates (Wan 2.2 T2V and I2V, LTX-2.5 T2V/I2V with audio, optionally HunyuanVideo 1.5 and H3), each with a placeholder map (prompt, seed, width/height, frames, input image). Uploading the reference image, `POST /prompt` with a client_id, listening on `/ws` (fall back to polling `/history`), then fetching outputs via `/view` maps directly onto the app's current poll-and-download loop. Built-in Node `fetch` and `WebSocket` (Node 22+) keep it zero-dependency.
  3. Optional Adapter C, Wan2GP: only via its Python API or MCP, so it is less natural for a Node app.
- **Batch mode must become client-side queueing.** None of these servers has an equivalent of OpenAI's Batch API (JSONL upload, 24 h SLA). The app would split prompts by blank line (as now), enqueue N jobs, throttle to the GPU's single-job concurrency, persist queue state locally so it survives restarts, and optionally fan out across several backend URLs (multiple GPUs or pods).
- **Privacy and security:** ComfyUI has no auth, so the app should default to 127.0.0.1 and recommend an SSH tunnel or a reverse proxy with auth for remote GPU boxes. Never expose 8188 publicly. The "key never on disk" principle maps to holding a cloud-pod token in memory only.
- ComfyUI port detection should probe both 8188 (portable) and 8000 (Desktop).

### Gaps
- The `/upload/image` route (needed for first-frame I2V) is on ComfyUI's routes page per my understanding, but this session did not confirm it from a fetched page.
- Pinokio was not researched (no time and no fetch access). It is a one-click installer launcher, not an API, so it is likely low relevance.
- No documented SwarmUI HTTP API for video batch jobs was found. SwarmUI may have a general API doc not surfaced by search.
- Whether SGLang Diffusion's Apple Silicon support covers video models at usable speed is unverified.
- I found no evidence of an "OpenAI-compatible video server" inside ComfyUI itself (for example, a custom node exposing `/v1/videos`). It may exist but was not found.

## Q4. Self-hosted cloud GPU option: cost per clip on RunPod, Modal, Vast.ai and AutoDL

### Takeaway
Self-managed rented GPUs bring Wan 2.2 or LTX clips down to **roughly $0.01–0.10 per 5 s 720p clip** (Vast.ai or AutoDL 4090/5090 at about $0.27–0.60/h, or ¥1.9–2.9/h, with 1–5 min generations). ### Inferences
- On a per-clip basis, **self-managed pods (Vast or AutoDL) are about 3–30× cheaper than hosted per-request APIs for the same open model**. The catch is operational: model download, environment setup and idle time are all billed. A pod is cheapest when it runs a full queue back to back, which is exactly what a batch-queue frontend does.
- For a Chinese-speaking maintainer and users, **AutoDL (¥1.9–2.9/h) plus a ComfyUI image** is the natural "cloud mode". The app would connect through an SSH tunnel or AutoDL's custom-service port to the pod's ComfyUI.
- RunPod Serverless or Modal suit sporadic single clips (scale to zero) but add cold-start minutes, because model weights are tens of GB. For bulk jobs, a persistent pod is cheaper.

### Gaps
- I found no first-party RunPod Serverless per-second GPU rate table for this session, and no measured per-clip cost for a self-deployed Wan 2.2 serverless worker.
- No Modal example exists for Wan 2.2, LTX-2.x or H3. The cost estimates above are extrapolated.
- No AutoDL-specific Wan 2.2 or LTX-2.5 benchmark was found. The cost per clip uses CSDN timings with unknown settings.
- H3 cloud cost per clip was not found (32 GB+ needed, so 5090 or 48–80 GB-class cards).

## Q5. Practical verdict: when does local or self-hosted beat paid APIs on cost, quality and time?

### Takeaway
Local and self-hosted open models are a **viable backend for Sora2App's core loop** (prompt in, optional first frame, queue, poll, MP4 to a local folder), with strong privacy and no per-video fee. The trade-offs are **lower quality than the closed leaders unless you accept H3's territorial license**, **much lower throughput** (about 20–30 clips per GPU-hour, not 50,000-request batches), and **an NVIDIA GPU (or a rented pod) as a hard requirement**, because the Mac is not a practical generator for Wan-class models. Local wins for a hobbyist or small studio that generates hundreds to thousands of short clips per month, already owns or rents a 24–32 GB NVIDIA GPU, values privacy and can tolerate Wan 2.2 or LTX-2.5 quality. Paid APIs still win for top-tier quality, long clips, occasional use, or strict deadlines on large batches.

### Inferences
- **Cost break-even (rough, with assumptions flagged).** Electricity for a 4090 at about 450–600 W system draw for about 3 min is about 0.025–0.03 kWh per clip, i.e. a few fen or cents. This is an assumption (power draw and tariff not researched). Against a $0.30/clip hosted price, local saves about $0.29 per clip. If a 24–32 GB GPU costs on the order of $1,500–3,000 (an assumption, not researched), hardware pays back after about **5,000–10,000 clips**. Someone who already owns the GPU breaks even immediately. Renting a pod (Vast or AutoDL) has no capex and costs about $0.01–0.10 per clip, so for most hobbyists **"rent a pod when needed, drive it from the app"** beats both buying a GPU and paying hosted APIs.
- **Time.** One local GPU delivers about 500–700 five-second clips per day (24 h at 2–3 min each). Batches above about 1,000 prompts need cloud fan-out (several pods), which the redesigned app could support by accepting multiple backend URLs.
- **Quality.** Users migrating from Sora 2 or Sora 2 Pro will find Wan 2.2 and LTX-2.5 noticeably weaker (AA Elo gap of about 200). H3 closes the gap but adds license, territory and 32 GB VRAM constraints. Native audio (a Sora 2 feature) is retained only with LTX-2.x or H3.
- **Mac strategy.** Because Sora2App ships as a macOS bundle, the realistic product shape is a **Mac app as controller/queue frontend plus a GPU backend elsewhere**: a LAN Windows/Linux PC running ComfyUI or SGLang, or an AutoDL, Vast or RunPod pod via SSH tunnel. An "experimental on-device" option could cover LTX-2.x via MLX on Macs with 64 GB or more.
- **Recommended targets (priority order):** (1) ComfyUI adapter with Wan 2.2 (I2V and T2V, with LightX2V 4-step) and LTX-2.5 templates, the broadest compatibility, Desktop-friendly; (2) OpenAI-compatible `/v1/videos` adapter (SGLang Diffusion or vLLM-Omni), nearly free to add since it mirrors the old Sora client; (3) H3 as an opt-in template with a license warning. Defer Wan2GP, Draw Things and SwarmUI integrations (no suitable HTTP batch API confirmed).
- **Vendor-risk argument.** Open weights cannot be "shut down" once downloaded (unlike Sora's 2026-09-24 shutdown), but upgrades are not guaranteed, as the Wan 2.5–3.0 closures show. Architecting for multiple backends hedges both directions.

### Gaps
- Real GPU purchase prices and power draw were not researched, so the break-even figures above are illustrative.
- No head-to-head quality study (human preference) between Sora 2 and the open models at equal resolution was found. AA leaderboard Elo is the only proxy.
- I found no survey data on how many hobbyists or small studios actually own 24 GB or larger NVIDIA GPUs versus Macs, so the share of Sora2App users who could run local generation is unknown.
